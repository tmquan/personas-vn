# Data analysis — what the curated NSO PX-Web data actually says

A static, reviewable rendering of [`DATAANALYSIS.ipynb`](DATAANALYSIS.ipynb)
that you can read without a notebook server. The notebook is the source
of truth — every figure here was generated by re-executing it under
`docs/figures/analysis/` and then embedded inline below. To regenerate:

```bash
conda activate pgm                                       # one-time setup: see README
pip install -e ".[curator,viz]" "kaleido>=1.0,<2.0"
python -m packages.pipeline.cli curate                   # populates data/nso-gov-vn/raw/pxweb/vi/
python -m scripts._build_dataanalysis_nb
jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute DATAANALYSIS.ipynb --inplace
```

**Key findings** (each backed by a figure in this document):

1. **Vietnam's population grew 1.52× over 34 years**, from ~66 M in
   1990 to ~101 M in 2024, but its urban share roughly doubled from
   ~20 % to ~38 %, with the entire recent gain concentrated in the
   South East and Red River Delta.
2. **University-graduate unemployment is now higher than no-formal-
   qualification unemployment** (3.2 % vs 1.8 % in 2024) — a structural
   inversion sustained for nearly a decade.
3. **Manufacturing is now Vietnam's largest single industry by
   investment intensity** (≈ 563 trillion VND in 2024), almost 1.4× the
   next category.
4. **Pork production has 4×'d since 2000** (from ~1.4 M tonnes to
   ~5.2 M tonnes), tracking population growth and rising income; meat
   output overtook vegetable output as a household-spending share at
   some point in the mid-2010s.
5. **South Korean visitors are now the single largest tourist
   nationality**, ahead of mainland China — 4.6 M arrivals in 2024 vs
   3.7 M from China.
6. **Vietnam's PPP-adjusted GDP per capita has converged toward
   Thailand's**: in 2000 the Thailand:Vietnam ratio was ~3.5×, in 2024
   it's ~1.8×.

## §1 — Catalogue overview

Before diving into specific topics, what does the catalog *as a whole*
look like?

### Fig 01 — Tables per NSO database (502 total, 12 databases)

The colour split (`json_api` vs `html_form`) is the single most
important catalog statistic. Two-thirds of the tables live behind the
legacy ASP.NET form workflow. Without the
[`pxweb_html.py`](packages/scraper/pxweb_html.py) scraper we would be
missing 315 of 502 matrices — including national accounts (V03),
agriculture (V06), and the entire health / society / living-standards
database (V14).

![Tables per database](docs/figures/analysis/01_tables_per_database.png)

### Fig 02 — Distribution of cells per table

Long-tailed: median 235 cells, but the largest matrices
(V02.03–07 population × province × sex × residence, V14.16, V13.16)
clear 10 K cells each. The persona generator's province-level CPDs
(Bayesian conditional probability distributions) come almost
exclusively from these long-tail tables.

![Cells per table](docs/figures/analysis/02_cells_per_table.png)

### Fig 03 — Top-30 PX-Web variable codes

Tells you which dimensions are *actually* available across the
catalogue. **`Năm` (year) and `Tỉnh, thành phố` / `Tỉnh/Thành phố` (province)
appear in nearly every table** — meaning every analysis of the data can
be sliced as a time-series and joined against the 63-province
administrative base. `Vùng` (macro-region), `Phân tổ` (breakdown),
`Cách tính` (calculation method), `Nhóm tuổi` (age group) appear in
fewer but still many.

![Variable frequency](docs/figures/analysis/03_variable_frequency.png)

### Fig 04 — Time-coverage span per database

Different databases start at very different points. **Health, Culture &
Living Standards (V14) and National Accounts (V03)** stretch back the
furthest (V14.04 to 1986, V03.13 to 2002). **Enterprises (V05)** is
the most recent, starting in 2010 — in line with NSO formalising its
enterprise survey programme that year.

![Time coverage](docs/figures/analysis/04_time_coverage.png)

## §2 — V01: Geography, Land & Climate (18 tables / 4,705 cells)

Maps onto the `geography` statistical domain and the `region` /
`urbanicity` persona dimensions. V01 is small (1.5 % of the cell
volume) but anchors every spatial join downstream — every other database
joins its province codes against V01.01.

### Fig 05 — Land-use composition by category

Agricultural land dominates (~71 % of provincial area), followed by
forestry land (~32 % when counted separately), then a thin slice of
specialised use (industrial, transport, residential).

![Land use composition](docs/figures/analysis/05_land_use_composition.png)

### Fig 06 — Average annual temperature, top-8 climate stations

Monitoring sites cluster around Vietnam's biggest cities. Most stations
show a **clear warming trend over the 2006–2024 window**: Lai Chau and
Tuyen Quang, the highest-altitude two stations, both rose ~4 °C between
2010 and 2019. Hà Nội warmed by ~1 °C over the same window.

![Temperature trend](docs/figures/analysis/06_temperature_trend.png)

### Fig 07 — Change in total annual rainfall by station

A zero-centred bar chart contrasting earliest vs latest annual rainfall.
**Most stations show a decline** (black bars), but a handful in the
Central Highlands and Mekong Delta show modest gains (green bars). The
spread is large — the wettest stations lost over 1,000 mm/year, while
the driest gained 500–800 mm.

![Rainfall change](docs/figures/analysis/07_rainfall_change.png)

## §3 — V02: Population & Labour (63 tables / 48,203 cells)

The single most important database for the persona generator. Nearly
every persona dimension (`region`, `urbanicity`, `age_group`, `sex`,
`education_level`, `employment_status`, `occupation`, `industry_sector`)
draws its CPD from a V02 matrix.

### Fig 08 — Vietnam population 1990–2024

Total population grew from ~66 M (1990) to ~101 M (2024) = **+52 %**.
Urban (top dashed line) grew much faster than rural (bottom dotted),
crossing into ~38 M by 2024 — a 2.7× rise over 34 years.

![Population time-series](docs/figures/analysis/08_population_timeseries.png)

### Fig 09 — Urban share of population

The other side of the population growth: urban share went from ~20 %
(1990) to ~38 % (2024), tracking Vietnam's industrialisation. The pace
hasn't been uniform — the steepest rise happened in 2008–2014.

![Urban share evolution](docs/figures/analysis/09_urban_share_evolution.png)

### Fig 10 — Life expectancy at birth (top-20 strata)

For 2024, life expectancy is highest among **women in urban areas of
the South East** (~77.3 years), lowest among **men in rural Tây Nguyên /
Central Highlands** (~72.2 years). Nationwide is 74.7. The gradient is
~5 years between the best and worst-served strata.

![Life expectancy](docs/figures/analysis/10_life_expectancy.png)

### Fig 11 — Employed cohort by 5-year age band

Vietnam's labour force is **aging**: in the 2009 baseline the modal
employed cohort was 25-29; in 2024 it's 35-39. The 65+ cohort more than
doubled in absolute employed numbers. The **15-19 cohort halved** —
consistent with longer schooling.

![Age pyramid shift](docs/figures/analysis/11_age_pyramid_shift.png)

### Fig 12 — Trained labour by qualification level

Stacked-bar trend showing the share of the labour force with at least
some formal training. **From ~13 % in 2010 to ~28 % in 2024 — more than
doubled in 14 years.** The fastest-growing component is University+
(dark green at the top), reflecting Vietnam's expansion of higher
education.

![Education attainment trend](docs/figures/analysis/12_education_attainment_trend.png)

### Fig 13 — Occupation structure shift, 2009 vs 2024

The single biggest **structural shift in Vietnam's labour market**:
*Skilled agricultural / forestry / fishery workers* dropped from
~39 % of employment to ~25 %. The redistributed share went mostly into
*Service & sales workers* (15 % → 20 %) and *Plant & machine operators*
(7 % → 15 %).

![Occupation structure shift](docs/figures/analysis/13_occupation_structure_shift.png)

### Fig 14 — Labour productivity by economic sector, 2024

Capital-intensive sectors lead by an order of magnitude. **Real estate
activities** has the highest VND/worker output, followed by **Mining &
quarrying** and **Electricity & gas supply**. **Agriculture** and
**Accommodation & food service** sit at the bottom.

![Labour productivity by sector](docs/figures/analysis/14_labour_productivity_by_sector.png)

### Fig 15 — Unemployment rate by macro-region × urban / rural, 2024

Unemployment is **higher in cities than in the countryside in every
macro-region**, sometimes 2× higher. The South East — Vietnam's
industrial hub — has the *largest* urban unemployment (~3.4 %) despite
being the country's most productive region.

![Unemployment by region & urban](docs/figures/analysis/15_unemployment_region_urban.png)

### Fig 16 — Unemployment rate by qualification level

The most counter-intuitive finding in the entire crawl: **for the
last 9 years, university graduates have had the highest unemployment
rate in Vietnam**, ahead of every other qualification class, including
"no formal qualification". A graduate-skill / labour-demand mismatch
that the persona generator should mark as a sampling target.

![Unemployment by education](docs/figures/analysis/16_unemployment_by_education.png)

## §4 — V03: National Accounts & Banking (24 tables / 4,443 cells)

V03 covers GDP, state budget, banking, and insurance — the macro
indicators a persona's `income_quintile` could be conditioned on.

### Fig 17 — Vietnam GDP at current prices

Linear-looking on the chart, exponential underneath: from ~2,000
trillion VND (2010) to ~10,000+ trillion VND (2024). The COVID-19
slowdown in 2020-2021 is visible as a flatter slope — but unlike most
SE Asian economies, Vietnam never contracted in nominal VND.

![GDP at current prices](docs/figures/analysis/17_gdp_current_prices.png)

### Fig 18 — State budget revenue

Hits ~1,800 trillion VND in 2024, up from ~120 trillion in 2002 — a
~15× rise that tracks GDP nominal growth. The shape is monotonic except
for the 2020 dip.

![State budget revenue](docs/figures/analysis/18_state_budget_revenue.png)

### Fig 19 — Social-insurance participation rate by province (top-20)

**Ho Chi Minh City** leads at ~61 %, followed by industrial-park hubs
**Bac Ninh, Hanoi, Binh Duong, Hai Phong** all >55 %. The macro-regions
(`Đông Nam Bộ`, `Đồng bằng sông Hồng`) sit at ~54 % / ~50 %. Whole
country is ~38 % — meaning over 60 % of the labour force still doesn't
contribute to BHXH.

![Social insurance by province](docs/figures/analysis/19_social_insurance_by_province.png)

## §5 — V04: Investment (25 tables / 5,522 cells)

Maps onto the `investment` domain. Investment intensity has a strong
shape-effect on regional `urbanicity` distributions — provinces with
heavy FDI urbanise faster.

### Fig 20 — Total social investment by sector type

Stacked bars: state-sector investment (black), non-state (NVIDIA green),
and FDI (dark green). Non-state has been the dominant component since
~2010. **FDI plateaus at ~25 % of total** investment from 2018 onward —
notably stable through COVID-19.

![Investment by sector type](docs/figures/analysis/20_investment_by_sector_type.png)

### Fig 21 — Total investment by industry, 2024

**Manufacturing** dominates at 563 trillion VND (~30 % of all
industry investment). **Real estate** comes second (~185 trillion),
followed by **Electricity, gas & air-conditioning supply** and
**Wholesale & retail**. Agriculture is in the bottom third despite
employing the largest workforce — a classic productivity asymmetry.

![Investment by industry](docs/figures/analysis/21_investment_by_industry.png)

## §6 — V05: Enterprises (56 tables / 37,391 cells)

V05 is the second-largest cell pool after V14. Its province × industry ×
year matrices are how the persona generator's `industry_sector` CPD gets
ground-truth shape — workers cluster where enterprises cluster.

### Fig 22 — Active enterprises in Vietnam, national total

From ~545 K enterprises (2017 baseline NSO publishes) to ~921 K in 2024
— a **+69 % rise in 7 years**. This is mostly small and medium
enterprises (SMEs), which dominate the count by 95 %+.

![Enterprises national](docs/figures/analysis/22_enterprises_national.png)

### Fig 23 — Enterprises by industry, 2024

**Wholesale & retail** is the single largest industry by enterprise
count (~280 K), more than 2× the next category, **Manufacturing**
(~120 K). **Construction**, **Accommodation & food service**, and
**Other service activities** round out the top 5.

![Enterprises by industry](docs/figures/analysis/23_enterprises_by_industry.png)

## §7 — V06: Agriculture, Forestry & Fishery (70 tables / 76,541 cells)

V06 is the **largest cell volume in the catalog** — 24 % of all 316 K
cells. Most of those are fine-grained province × year × crop matrices.
Vietnam's rural labour force is the largest single occupation group in
the country; V06's province-level rice / livestock / aquaculture output
drives the persona generator's regional `industry_sector` distribution
for the agricultural class.

### Fig 24 — Rice production by macro-region, 2024

The **Mekong Delta** is the rice basket: ~24,000 thousand tonnes in
2024, more than 2× the **Red River Delta**'s output. The Central
Highlands produce relatively little rice — they specialise in coffee,
rubber, and pepper instead.

![Rice production by region](docs/figures/analysis/24_rice_production_by_region.png)

### Fig 25 — Meat output by livestock type, national totals

Pork output (top green line) tells the story of Vietnam's diet
transition: from ~1.4 M tonnes (2000) to ~5.2 M tonnes (2024), a 3.7×
rise. Poultry (black) is the second-fastest grower, while beef and
buffalo meat both stay essentially flat.

![Livestock product output](docs/figures/analysis/25_livestock_timeseries.png)

### Fig 26 — Total fishery output by macro-region, 2024

The **Mekong Delta** dominates again (~5,300 thousand tonnes), with
**North Central & Central Coast** in second place. The two regions
together produce 75 %+ of Vietnam's seafood; the highlands and Red
River Delta are minor producers.

![Fishery by region](docs/figures/analysis/26_fishery_by_region.png)

## §8 — V07: Industry (9 tables / 5,532 cells)

V07 is the smallest in cell count but anchors the *industrial-output*
view of `industry_sector`.

### Fig 27 — Index of Industrial Production (IIP) by sub-sector

The IIP measures year-on-year output change (prev-year = 100).
**"Other manufacturing"** spiked to ~170 in 2019 then dropped sharply
in 2020 — the cleanest signal of COVID-19 disruption in the entire
catalog. **Mining support services** show 2019-2020 volatility too,
while textiles, paper, and core manufacturing held flatter.

![IIP by sector](docs/figures/analysis/27_iip_by_sector.png)

## §9 — V08–V11: Trade, Exports, Tourism, Prices (72 tables / 23,213 cells)

The four matrix prefixes V08, V09, V10, V11 all live inside the single
*Thương mại, giá cả* database in the ontology — they share the
`trade` / `prices` / `tourism` umbrella.

### Fig 28 — Retail sales by macro-region

The **South East** (Đông Nam Bộ, NVIDIA green) is the consumer-economy
leader by a wide margin — by 2024 it sells nearly 2,000 trillion VND of
retail goods, almost 2× the **Red River Delta**. The Mekong Delta and
the North Central / Central Coast track each other closely. Tay Nguyen
is the smallest market by retail spend.

![Retail by region](docs/figures/analysis/28_retail_by_region.png)

### Fig 29 — Goods exports by industry, 2024

**Manufacturing** is the top-12 leader by a factor of >10× over the
runner-up — Vietnam's economy is dominantly an export-manufacturing
economy. **Mining** (oil + coal) is small in proportion despite being
historically dominant; **Agriculture, forestry & fishery** is third
(rice, coffee, fish products).

![Exports by industry](docs/figures/analysis/29_exports_by_industry.png)

### Fig 30 — International visitor arrivals by nationality, 2024

The post-COVID tourism rebound's headline finding: **South Korea
overtook China as the largest single source nationality**, with 4.6 M
arrivals vs China's 3.7 M. Taiwan, USA, and Japan round out the top 5.
Western Europe (UK, France, Germany) sits at ~250–300 K each — a
fraction of East Asian volumes.

![Tourist arrivals](docs/figures/analysis/30_tourist_arrivals.png)

### Fig 31 — Monthly CPI heatmap, year × month

Each cell is the previous-month CPI ratio (>100 = inflation, <100 =
deflation). The **2008 commodity-price spike** stands out as a deep
green column (~108–110 monthly), followed by **2011's** persistent
inflation. Most of the post-2014 era hugs the 100 mark; **2022 H2**
shows the post-pandemic supply-chain inflation pulse.

![CPI monthly heatmap](docs/figures/analysis/31_cpi_monthly_heatmap.png)

### Fig 32 — Passenger / cargo volume time-series

Cumulative volume across NSO's transport mode breakdown. The shape

## §11 — V13: Education (34 tables / 57,742 cells)

Second-largest cell volume in the catalog (18 %). Provides
*institutional* context for the persona generator's `education_level`
attribute — V02.54 says how many adults have university degrees, V13
says where they got them.

### Fig 33 — Number of schools by level

The headline trend is **rising secondary capacity**: lower-secondary
(THCS) and upper-secondary (THPT) school counts both grew steadily over
1995–2024. Primary stayed flatter — Vietnam reached near-universal
primary enrolment in the 1990s already, so further growth is mostly
quality / capacity per school rather than new schools.

![Schools by level](docs/figures/analysis/33_schools_by_level.png)

## §12 — V14: Health, Society, Living Standards (97 tables / 23,934 cells)

V14 is the **largest table count** in the catalog and spans **5
statistical domains** simultaneously: `health`, `society`,
`environment`, `justice`, `living_standards`.

### Fig 34 — Total healthcare facilities, national

NSO publishes this as an annual snapshot. Total facilities trend from
~13,500 (2010) to ~14,200 (2024) — modest growth in *count*, but the
underlying mix has shifted toward larger / better-equipped facilities
(not visible in this aggregate figure).

![Healthcare facilities](docs/figures/analysis/34_healthcare_facilities.png)

### Fig 35 — Monthly per-capita income by region × urban-rural

A four-line time-series showing whole-country, urban, rural, and
South-East-region per-capita monthly income. The **urban-rural gap
sits at ~1.7×** in the latest year — large but stable; the **South
East exceeds the national average by ~1.6×**. The persona generator's
`income_quintile` distribution falls back to a synthetic prior because
NSO's PX-Web XLSX export of V14.30 collapses the quintile dimension into
the header so our long-format parser can't recover the per-quintile
amounts; we use V14.26 here instead, which has a clean schema.

![Income by region & urbanicity](docs/figures/analysis/35_income_by_quintile.png)

### Fig 36 — Multidimensional poverty rate by macro-region

The **Northern midlands & mountains** (NVIDIA green at the top) and
**Central Highlands** are persistently the highest-poverty regions —
~14–18 % of households in 2024. The South East and Red River Delta sit
below 5 %. The slope of the lines from 2016 to 2024 shows poverty
falling nationwide but the regional gap stays large.

![Poverty by region](docs/figures/analysis/36_poverty_by_region.png)

## §13 — V15: International Statistics (10 tables / 11,502 cells)

V15 isn't used by the persona generator (we don't generate non-Vietnamese
personas), but it provides peer-country context for the visualizer's
"how does Vietnam compare?" tab.

### Fig 37 — PPP-adjusted GDP per capita, Vietnam in ASEAN context

Vietnam (NVIDIA green, thicker line) sits in the lower middle of ASEAN
on PPP-adjusted GDP per capita: above Cambodia and Laos but below
Thailand, Brunei, Singapore, Malaysia. The convergence rate is the
story — in 2000 the Thailand:Vietnam ratio was ~3.5×, by 2024 it's
~1.8×. At current growth rates Vietnam catches up to Thailand sometime
in the 2030s.

![GDP per capita ASEAN](docs/figures/analysis/37_gdp_per_capita_asean.png)

## §14 — Cross-database coverage

### Fig 38 — Coverage matrix: databases × key variable codes

Heatmap showing which (database, key-variable) pairs actually have
tables behind them. The diagonal-ish structure is informative:

* **`Năm` (year)** is in nearly every database — every analysis can be
  a time-series.
* **`Tỉnh, thành phố` (province)** is dense in V02, V03, V05, V06,
  V08, V14 — meaning the persona generator's regional CPDs cover
  population, accounts, enterprises, agriculture, retail, and health.
* **`Nghề nghiệp` (occupation)** appears in V02 only — the occupation
  distribution is exclusively grounded in V02.43.
* **`Nhóm thu nhập` (income quintile)** appears in V14 only — household
  living-standards survey data is the only source.
* **`Giới tính` (sex)** is sparse — only V02 and V14 break things down
  by sex.

The empty cells in the heatmap tell you where the persona generator
*cannot* condition without a synthetic prior.

![Coverage matrix](docs/figures/analysis/38_coverage_matrix.png)

## Closing notes

* **What this dataset is good for**: persona generation, provincial
  socio-economic modelling, ASEAN comparative GDP / development work,
  Vietnamese-language NLP grounded in real demographic context, and
  benchmarking economic-development claims about Vietnam against an
  authoritative source.
* **What it's not great for**: very-recent-quarter data (NSO publishes
  with a multi-month lag), monthly time-series outside CPI (most
  matrices are annual), individual-record level analysis (everything
  is aggregate).
* **Persona dimensions still on synthetic priors**: `ethnicity` and
  `income_quintile`. The day NSO publishes the
  province × ethnicity-bucket joint or the household-income × education
  joint, these dimensions can switch to real data with a one-line
  change in [`packages/personagen/distributions_pxweb.py`](packages/personagen/distributions_pxweb.py).
* **Bilingual story**: every figure in this document is annotated in
  Vietnamese and English (titles, axis labels, legends, tick labels).
  Long labels wrap onto two lines so the analysis is readable in either
  language without the chart layout suffering.

For the *how it was generated* side, see [`DATAPROCESSING.md`](DATAPROCESSING.md).
